# Revision pass

One tightening pass between finishing a draft and running the checker. It is a
drafting step, not an extra lint loop — the 3-pass cap in SKILL.md is
unchanged. Procedure from Lanham, *Revising Prose* (1979), the Paramedic
Method; it targets exactly the register agents drift into: preposition chains,
copulas, nominalizations, slow wind-ups.

Per paragraph:

1. Mark the prepositions (*of, in, for, by, into, onto*). Three or more in one
   clause means the action is hiding in a noun.
2. Mark the *to be* forms (is, are, was, were, be, been, being).
3. Ask: where's the action? Who is doing it?
4. Put that actor in the subject and the action in a simple active verb.
   "Validation of the manifest is performed by the loader" → "The loader
   validates the manifest."
5. Delete the slow wind-up (`It is important to note that…`, `The purpose of
   this section is to…`) — start where the content starts.
6. Delete redundancy; keep every epistemic hedge (see SKILL.md step 2).

Then two checks on the whole draft:

- **Lard factor** — the one computable metric in the canon:
  `(words before − words after) / words before`. On first drafts of
  institutional prose Lanham treats ~30–50% as normal. If your pass removed
  under ~10%, you skimmed; do one paragraph honestly and re-measure.
- **Read aloud** (Le Guin, *Steering the Craft*). Not for beauty: reading
  aloud catches clause pile-ups, subjects severed from verbs by edits, and
  antecedents that stopped pointing at anything. If you can't finish a
  sentence in one breath without backtracking, restructure it.
