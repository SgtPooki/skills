# Platform norms: Hacker News, LinkedIn, Product Hunt, Reddit

Channel culture overrides generic copy rules — HN actively punishes what the
persuasion canon optimizes for. Load this before promoting on any of these
platforms. HN rules are grounded in the official Show HN guidelines.

## Hacker News (Show HN)

- **Only post a Show HN for something people can actually try.** Official rule: "Show HN is for something you've made that other people can play with" — blog posts, signup pages, waitlists, and landing pages are explicitly off topic.
- **Remove signup/email walls from the try-it path before posting.** "Make it easy for users to try your thing out, ideally without barriers such as signups or emails."
- **Don't post until it's usable** — a premature launch burns the one first-impression slot.
- **Title format: `Show HN: <Name> – <plain factual description>`.** No superlatives, exclamation points, caps-for-emphasis, emoji, or gratuitous "10x faster"/"#1" numbers — mods rewrite or flag engineered titles.
- **Skip version-bump posts** unless the release is a major overhaul.
- **Write in technical plain-speak; delete every marketing-register word.** "Revolutionary", "seamless", "empower", and feature-benefit copy structures get flagged and downvoted. The audience rewards "it does X by doing Y" over persuasion.
- **Post a first comment with: backstory, how it works technically, what's unusual about the approach, and known limitations.** Admitting limitations up front reads as competence.
- **Disclose that it's yours; discuss tradeoffs when asked.** Undisclosed affiliation, when discovered, sinks the account.
- **Answer questions all day; never reply defensively.** A present, humble, technically substantive submitter keeps the thread alive; snapping at criticism reliably turns it hostile.
- **Never solicit upvotes or comments — not even from friends.** The voting-ring detector penalizes the post and can shadow-kill the account.

## LinkedIn

- **Put the strongest specific line first — the fold cuts at roughly 2–3 lines (~200 chars) before "…see more".**
- **1–2 sentence paragraphs with white space**; most reading happens on mobile.
- **Keep external links out of the post body** — link posts get roughly half the reach or worse; LinkedIn favors native content.
- **Treat "link in first comment" as contested, not reliable** — reports differ on whether the workaround still preserves reach. Prefer native value in the post, link as secondary path.
- **Personal narrative is welcome** — first person, a real decision or failure, a concrete lesson tied to a professional point. But "I'm humbled to announce…" templates are now widely mocked.
- **Connection notes: ≤300 chars (200 on some free accounts); aim ~120–180.** One specific reason to connect, no pitch.
- **Never pitch in the connection note or the first message after accepting** — pitch-slapping is the platform's most complained-about behavior.
- **Do comment-led outreach:** substantive comments on prospects' posts before any ask; the current algorithm also weights comment activity heavily.
- **Skip engagement pods and generic AI-sounding copy** — both explicitly deprioritized.

## Product Hunt

- **Tagline ≤60 characters, plain, benefit-stated, no hyperbole or emoji** — say what it does for whom.
- **Description leads with the problem and the concrete differentiator**, not adjectives; unverifiable claims blur into the day's other dozen launches.
- **Write the maker/first comment before launch day and post it immediately:** who you are → why you built it (the pain) → what's different → a specific ask for feedback.
- **Ask for feedback on something specific, never for upvotes** (soliciting upvotes is prohibited).
- **Reply to every comment quickly with substance** — comment velocity and depth are ranking signals; first hours matter most.
- **Launch midweek (Tue–Thu); treat launch day as a full-day commitment.**
- Rewarded: honest origin stories, transparent pricing, admitting what's missing. Punished: bought upvotes, team praise-swarms, marketing speak.
- **Expectation caveat:** PH's weight has declined — much of the audience is other makers/marketers, and upvote-service spam has diluted rankings. Treat it as a backlink/credibility/launch-asset play more than a customer channel.

## Reddit

- **Obey the spirit of the 10:1 reddiquette guideline:** no more than ~1 in 10 of your posts/comments should benefit your own product; accounts over that ratio are treated as spammers, up to shadowban.
- **Read the target subreddit's rules first — they override everything.** Some ban promotion outright, some gate it to weekly threads, some require flair.
- **Disclose affiliation in the first line** ("Full disclosure: I built this"). Discovered deception → ban; transparency measurably helps.
- **Contribute genuinely in a subreddit before promoting in it** — an account whose history is only its own product is the textbook removal case.
- **Post value-with-context, never drive-by links:** a text post explaining the problem, what you built, how it works, and what you want feedback on survives; a bare URL is removed almost everywhere.
- **Never use multiple accounts, vote manipulation, or friend-brigades** — sitewide bannable.
- Dev-tool subreddit notes: **r/programming** is effectively hostile to promotion (only deeply technical write-ups where the product is incidental survive); **r/webdev** gates project show-offs to Showoff Saturday, technical detail first; **r/selfhosted** is comparatively permissive for self-hostable projects when the developer speaks as a developer (stack, license, roadmap, honest limitations).
- **Answer every question in your thread, including hostile ones, without defensiveness.**
